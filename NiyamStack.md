
# SECTION 1 — UNDERSTAND THE BUSINESS: NIYAMSTACK SOURCE

## 1. What is this product/business?

**Niyamstack Source is a procurement management platform.**

Its purpose is to digitise and organise the process through which an organisation:

**finds vendors → requests quotations → receives quotations → compares them → gets approval → creates purchase records → manages contracts → evaluates vendors.**

The brochure specifically describes Source as:

> **“A structured procurement and vendor management platform.”** 

It is intended for organisations such as:

* NGOs
* Hotels
* Schools
* Hospitals
* Event organisations
* Other organisations that regularly purchase goods/services from vendors

The core problem it addresses is **unstructured procurement**—where vendor information, quotations, approvals and purchasing decisions may be scattered across Excel, email, WhatsApp and paper-based processes.

### In simple terms

Niyamstack Source is trying to turn this:

**Vendor contacts + WhatsApp + Email + Excel + Paper + Manual approvals**

into this:

**One central procurement system**

# 2. Who are the users/stakeholders?

The main stakeholders are:

### Internal users

**1. Procurement team**

* Creates purchase requirements
* Finds/selects vendors
* Creates RFQs
* Collects quotations
* Compares quotations
* Tracks procurement status

**2. Approvers / Management**

* Reviews quotations
* Approves or rejects purchases
* Reviews procurement spending
* Monitors vendor performance

**3. Finance team**

* Handles/monitors payments
* Tracks purchase-related financial information
* Verifies financial documentation

**4. Department/requesting employee**

* Raises a requirement
* Specifies what is needed
* Tracks procurement status

### External users

**5. Vendors/Suppliers**

* Register with the organisation
* Submit company information/documents
* Receive RFQs
* Submit quotations
* Participate in procurement
* Maintain contractual information

### Other stakeholders

* Organisation administrators
* Auditors
* Contract managers
* Senior management

So the basic relationship is:

**Employee/Department → Procurement → Vendor → Approver/Management → Finance**

# 3. Describe the current workflow

Based on the problem statement in the Niyamstack brochure, the existing process can be understood as a **fragmented/manual procurement workflow**. The brochure identifies Excel, paper, WhatsApp and disconnected tools as sources of operational problems. 

A typical current workflow would look like:

### Step 1 — Requirement

A department needs something.

For example:

> “We need 50 laptops.”

The requirement may be communicated through email, WhatsApp or a paper/internal request.

### Step 2 — Find vendors

The procurement employee searches existing contacts or finds new suppliers.

Vendor information may be stored in:

* Excel
* Email
* Phone contacts
* Previous documents
* WhatsApp

### Step 3 — Request quotations

Procurement contacts multiple vendors and asks them for quotations.

This may happen through:

**Email / WhatsApp / phone**

### Step 4 — Receive quotations

Different vendors send their quotations in different formats:

* PDF
* Excel
* Email
* WhatsApp
* Physical document

### Step 5 — Compare

The procurement employee manually compares:

* Price
* Quantity
* Delivery time
* Vendor
* Terms
* Other requirements

### Step 6 — Approval

The comparison is sent to the appropriate manager/authority for approval.

### Step 7 — Purchase

After approval, the organisation proceeds with the selected vendor.

### Step 8 — Contract/document management

Relevant contracts and vendor documents need to be stored and tracked.

### Step 9 — Vendor performance

The organisation needs to evaluate whether the vendor performed well.

### Step 10 — Renewal/follow-up

Contracts or vendor relationships may need renewal or follow-up.

# 6. Key learnings from competitors

For your business-understanding exercise, these are the most important things to learn from procurement competitors.

### 1. Procurement should be workflow-driven

A good procurement system shouldn't simply store vendors and quotations.

It should guide the user through:

**Requirement → RFQ → Quotations → Comparison → Approval → Purchase**

This is already reflected in Niyamstack Source's design. 

---

### 2. Vendor management should be central

A vendor shouldn't just be a name in a contact list.

The system should maintain the vendor's complete history:

**Vendor → Documents → Quotations → Purchases → Contracts → Performance → Renewal**

This gives management a complete picture of the supplier.

---

### 3. Approval workflows are critical

Procurement involves spending organisational money.

Therefore, the system needs:

**Request → Approval → Purchase**

with clear visibility into:

* Who requested it?
* Who approved it?
* When was it approved?
* What amount was approved?
* Which vendor was selected?
* Why was the vendor selected?

---

### 4. Quotation comparison is a key value proposition

One of the strongest opportunities for Source is reducing the manual effort involved in comparing vendor quotations.

Instead of:

**Vendor A PDF + Vendor B Excel + Vendor C Email**

the system should allow procurement staff to compare them in one place.

For example:

| Criteria      |    Vendor A |    Vendor B |            Vendor C |
| ------------- | ----------: | ----------: | ------------------: |
| Price         |   ₹5,00,000 |   ₹4,80,000 |           ₹5,20,000 |
| Delivery      |     10 days |     15 days |              7 days |
| Warranty      |      1 year |     2 years |              1 year |
| Payment terms | 50% advance | 30% advance | 100% after delivery |

This makes the procurement decision **transparent and auditable**.

---

### 5. Documents should be connected to business entities

Instead of keeping documents in random folders, Source should associate them with the relevant entity:

**Vendor**
→ Vendor documents

**RFQ**
→ RFQ documents

**Quotation**
→ Quotation document

**Purchase**
→ Purchase documents

**Contract**
→ Contract document

This makes retrieval much easier.

---

### 6. Reminders can prevent business losses

The brochure specifically mentions **renewal reminders** for Source. 

This can be expanded into automated reminders for:

* Contract expiry
* Vendor document expiry
* Pending quotation
* Pending approval
* Delayed delivery
* Pending payment
* Vendor evaluation

This moves Source from being merely a **record-keeping system** to an **active workflow management system**.
